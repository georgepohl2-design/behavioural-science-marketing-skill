# Worked example 2: Sport England, "This Girl Can" (2015)

**Input:** Sport England's 2015 launch campaign, by FCB Inferno. A 90-second TV and online film of real women (not models) running, swimming, boxing and dancing: wobbling, sweating, red-faced and loving it. Bold handwritten-style lines over the footage, such as "Sweating like a pig, feeling like a fox." Ends on "This Girl Can" and a website.

*A public, award-winning campaign, used to show the skill's output.*

---

## Verdict
A near-perfect unity and social proof campaign. It doesn't argue that exercise is good for you. It shows women like you already doing it, on their own terms. The weak spot is the step after the film: the ask is soft, so a lot of the goodwill has nowhere to go.

**The job:** behaviour-change brand film · **For:** women aged 14–40 who'd like to exercise but fear being judged · **The ask:** get active (and visit the site)

## Scorecard
| Principle | Rating | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Reciprocity | ⚠️ Weak | The film gives permission and a laugh, which counts. But there's no practical gift: no free class, no guide. |
| Commitment | ❌ Missing | No small first step. "Get active" is a big, vague ask. |
| Social proof | ✅ Strong | Real women of every shape, age and ability, all mid-exercise. "Women like me are already doing this." |
| Authority | ➖ Not needed | Rightly avoids experts. The audience's fear is judgement, not lack of information. |
| Liking | ✅ Strong | Funny, warm and honest. The lines sound like a mate, not a PE teacher. |
| Scarcity | ➖ Not needed | Urgency would add pressure to an audience held back by pressure. |
| Unity | ✅ Strong | "This Girl Can" is an identity, not a product. Women shared it as a badge, and the hashtag became a community. |

## 3 fixes
**1. Give a tiny first step at the end** (Commitment)
Turn "get active" into a 10-minute yes.
> Before: "This Girl Can" + website
> After: "This Girl Can. Find a beginners' session near you this week, no kit needed."

A small, specific first action makes the identity real, and the next session easier.
📖 **The story:** In 1966, homeowners who first agreed to a tiny "be a safe driver" window sign were far more likely to later accept a huge lawn billboard: about 76% against 17% asked cold. Small first steps lead to big ones.

**2. Give something useful, not just inspiring** (Reciprocity)
Partner with local centres for a free first class.
> After: "Your first session's on us. Just say 'This Girl Can' at the door."

Significant, personal and unexpected, and it gets her through the door.
📖 **The story:** In a 1971 experiment, people given a free can of Coke by a stranger bought about twice as many of his raffle tickets, whether they liked him or not. A small gift creates a strong pull to give something back, here a first visit.

**3. Get people to say it out loud** (Commitment + Unity)
Invite women to post their own "This Girl…" line.
> After: "This girl ___. Tell us yours #ThisGirlCan"

Public, active and freely chosen: the three things that make a commitment stick. And it feeds more social proof.
📖 **The story:** A restaurant cut no-shows from 30% to 10% by changing "Please call if you cancel" to "*Will* you please call if you cancel?" and waiting for the caller to say yes. When people say it themselves, they stick to it.

## Integrity check
Clean. The people are real, nothing is faked, and the tone empowers rather than shames. Sport England's tracking survey estimated 2.8 million women aged 14–40 became more active because of it.

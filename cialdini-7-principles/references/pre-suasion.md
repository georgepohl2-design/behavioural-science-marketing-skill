# Bonus: Pre-suasion, the moment before the ask

**In one line:** What people see, hear or think about just *before* your message changes how they respond to it.

## Why it works
Whatever has our attention right now feels more important. Cialdini calls this "privileged moments": channel attention towards the right idea, and the ask that follows lands better. It's the setup, not the pitch.

## The evidence
- **Clouds and coins** (Mandel & Johnson, 2002). On a furniture website, a background of fluffy clouds made shoppers care more about comfort and pick comfier sofas. A background of coins made them focus on price and pick cheaper ones. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1086/341573)
- **"Are you adventurous?"** (described in *Pre-Suasion*). Shoppers were asked for their email address to get a free sample of a new soft drink. When researchers first asked "Do you consider yourself adventurous?", sign-ups more than doubled, to about 76%. Almost everyone says yes to that question, and then acts like it.

## Use it in marketing and sales
- Set the frame first: open a landing page with the problem or goal you solve, then show the product.
- Ask a question that primes the trait you need: "Are you the kind of team that likes to test new ideas?"
- Choose the context: the media placement, imagery and words around your ad shape how it's read.
- On a sales call, open with the outcome they want before you show the price.

## Audit questions
1. What does the reader see or think about just before the ask?
2. Does that moment point them towards your strongest benefit?

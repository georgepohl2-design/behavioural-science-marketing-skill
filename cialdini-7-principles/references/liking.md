# 5. Liking: people say yes to people they like

**In one line:** We're far more likely to say yes to people we like, and we like people who are similar to us, who pay us genuine compliments, and who work with us towards the same goal.

## Why it works
Liking lowers our guard. Cialdini lists the things that make us like someone:
- **Similarity.** Same background, interests, problems, language.
- **Compliments.** Genuine praise, even when we suspect a motive.
- **Cooperation.** Being on the same side against a shared problem.
- **Familiarity.** Repeated, pleasant contact.
- **Association.** Being linked with things we already like.

## Stories from the books
- **"I like you."** Joe Girard, named by Guinness the world's greatest car salesman, sent every one of his 13,000-plus past customers a card every month with a simple message: "I like you." Cialdini's point: we're suckers for flattery, even when we know it's mass-produced.
- **Tupperware parties.** The real seller isn't the rep. It's your friend who's hosting. Cialdini found guests buy out of liking and obligation to the host far more than for the product.
- **Blaming the weather forecaster.** Cialdini describes TV weather presenters getting hate mail, and worse, for bad weather they only reported. We link messengers with their message, which is why brands pay to be linked with things we already love.

## The evidence
- **Negotiations** (reported by Cialdini, *Harvard Business Review*, 2001). Business-school students negotiating by email reached a deal about 90% of the time when they first swapped a bit of personal information, against about 55% when they went straight to business. [Article](https://hbr.org/2001/10/harnessing-the-science-of-persuasion)

## Famous examples
- **Compare the Market's meerkats** (VCCP, 2009). Turned a dull price comparison site into a brand people loved. Liking through character.
- **Dove Campaign for Real Beauty** (Ogilvy, 2004). Real women instead of models: liking through similarity.
- **Innocent Drinks.** Chatty, warm packaging copy that sounds like a friend.

## Use it in marketing
- Cast and write for similarity: show people who look, talk and work like your audience.
- Find a likeable brand voice and keep it consistent across every touchpoint.
- Use humour and warmth. Entertaining ads get liked, and liked ads get remembered.
- Partner with people your audience already likes: creators, communities, events.

## Use it in sales
- Find genuine common ground before business: same university, same old employer, same frustration.
- Give a specific, honest compliment about their work ("Your onboarding emails are the best I've seen in the sector").
- Frame it as you and them against the problem, not you against them.
- Mirror their language: use their words for their problem.

## Watch out
- Fake rapport ("I see you also like golf!") is obvious and kills trust.
- Celebrity ads with no link to the product get liked, but the brand gets forgotten.
- Liking alone doesn't close. Pair it with proof.

## Audit questions
1. Would the target audience see themselves in this?
2. Is there anything warm, human or entertaining, or is it all features?
3. Does it feel like "us against the problem"?
